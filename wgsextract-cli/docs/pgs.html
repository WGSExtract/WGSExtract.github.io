Local polygenic scores (research only)

Research use only; not a diagnosis. A polygenic score is a weighted sum of alleles, not an absolute disease risk. Do not change treatment or screening based on it. Clinical disease scores carry this warning too.

wgsextract score pgs --input sample.vcf.gz --build GRCh38 --trait height --download
wgsextract score pgs --input sample.vcf.gz --build GRCh38 --pgs-id PGS000018 --download --json

Genotypes stay on your machine. Only with --download is the score ID/build sent to the PGS Catalog, to download its metadata and harmonized weights. Without it, cached files are reused and missing public data causes an error naming --download. Explicit download permission also permits refreshing cached Catalog files. See privacy for destinations and persistent configuration. No scoring files are bundled or mirrored. Downloads retry transient failures three times. Each attempt checks the advertised byte count (when present) and validates the complete gzip stream or metadata JSON before replacing a cached file. Failed or truncated responses never replace a previously good copy. bcftools must already be on PATH; the command checks, it never installs tools.

Choosing a score#

--traitPGS IDCatalog trait

ldl-cholesterolPGS000065LDL cholesterol
prostate-cancerPGS000067Prostate cancer
lung-cancerPGS000070Lung cancer
thyroid-cancerPGS000087Thyroid cancer

This is a convenience list, not a clinical recommendation or a ranking of predictive accuracy. Any PGSnnnnnn is accepted with --pgs-id; an uncurated ID emits pgs_uncurated_score. A Catalog score whose authors set their own licence emits pgs_license_restricted with it, unless the licence only asks for attribution (CC BY 4.0). The bmi score, PGS005198, is the GIANT consortium's multi-ancestry score (Smit et al. 2025, Nat Med), under CC BY 4.0. It replaced PGS000027 (Khera et al. 2019), which is only "freely available to the academic community for research use" (#1165 compares the candidates). Review the publication, development ancestry, licence and limitations before using a score. A height score in raw score units is not height in centimetres; a disease score is not disease probability.

Your own additive scoring TSV (optionally gzipped) overrides Catalog selection:

wgsextract score pgs --input sample.vcf.gz --build GRCh38 \
  --scoring-file my_score.txt --min-match 0.9 --outdir scores

Persistent defaults go in config.toml:

pgs_min_match = 0.75
# pgs_keep_ambiguous = false
# pgs_scoring_file = "/path/to/my_score.txt.gz"

An explicit --scoring-file overrides the configured file. Custom files need #genome_build=GRCh38 (or GRCh37), and columns chr_name, chr_position (1-based), effect_allele, other_allele, effect_weight. Catalog harmonized files instead use hm_chr, hm_pos, and #HmPOS_build=...; the harmonized build takes precedence over their original build. Include #license=..., #citation=..., and #trait_reported=... in custom files. Unknown licence or citation is reported explicitly, never guessed.

Inputs and matching#

  • Choose the same build as the VCF. Only GRCh37 and GRCh38 are supported. Known chromosome-1 lengths inconsistent with --build are refused.
  • Select --sample NAME for a multi-sample VCF.
  • An explicit diploid genotype can match. Missing records, ./., site- or sample-filtered (FILTER or FORMAT/FT) calls, unresolved alleles and ambiguous duplicate records are unmatched. Missing never means homozygous reference by default, even in WGS.
  • Reference evidence: given a gVCF, or a variant VCF whose caller published a gVCF beside it (--reference-evidence gvcf, found through the caller's result manifest), a site is homozygous reference only where the gVCF calls it callable reference: enough depth and, where the gVCF has GQ, genotype quality (the confidence_* settings). Sites the gVCF calls uncovered, low_quality_reference, filtered, no_call, variant, excluded_by_policy or not_assessed stay missing, counted under that name. Sites the VCF doesn't list need an indexed --ref to check their alleles; --ref also gives the length of a contig the gVCF declares without one (otherwise its sites are not_assessed). See reference-evidence.md.
  • For a variants-only callset with a same-sample, same-build confident BED, add --confident-bed sample.confident.bed --ref reference.fa.gz. Only positions inside the BED may be inferred reference; positions outside it are unmatched. Explicit no-calls, filtered calls and overlapping deletions are never rescued by the BED. The BED is an assertion of callability, not just a list of capture targets.
  • The reference FASTA needs an existing .fai (also .gzi for BGZF); samtools faidx reference.fa.gz creates these. No reference is downloaded implicitly.
  • This first version scores additive autosomal SNVs only, on the forward strand, with exact allele matching. Indels, sex chromosomes, unresolved harmonization and multi-allelic effect definitions count as unmatched. Non-additive, haplotype and dosage-specific scoring models are refused. Palindromic A/T and C/G SNPs are also unmatched by default, as in pgsc_calc: coordinate harmonization alone cannot determine their strand. --keep-ambiguous (or pgs_keep_ambiguous = true) is an expert override only after independently verifying forward-strand scoring alleles; it emits a structured warning. --no-keep-ambiguous overrides that setting. There is no strand guessing, liftover, normalization, imputation or mean-imputation. Arrays and low-pass inputs are not qualified.

The native engine sums effect_weight × observed effect-allele count using NumPy in bounded batches. An on-disk genotype index keeps memory bounded even for large VCFs. Every scoring-file row counts in the match-rate denominator, including unsupported or unmapped rows. No matched variants produces a null score rather than a misleading zero.

--plink2-check optionally validates the same matched calls' arithmetic with an already-installed plink2, without mean-imputation. It is not an independent check of the matching policy. The JSON includes the PLINK sum, difference and text-rounding bound: PLINK's public score report uses six significant digits, so comparison allows that rounding plus 1e-6.

Outputs and percentiles#

The command writes <input>_<PGS ID>_pgs.json, .tsv, and a result manifest. The input stem is the same for .vcf, .vcf.gz, .vcf.bgz and .bcf; compression and variant-file extensions are not part of the output name. The JSON and manifest details include score sum, matched/total counts, match rate and reason counts, build, sample, scoring-file SHA-256, licence, citation and research warning. --json prints the usual result-manifest document for scripts; the typed API is api.score.pgs(api.ScorePgsParams(...)).

Ancestry-adjusted percentiles#

For the curated scores on GRCh38, score pgs also places the score against the HGDP+1KG reference samples, adjusted for the sample's genetic ancestry, the way the PGS Catalog Calculator (pgsc_calc, its pgscatalog.calc 0.3.1 ancestry step) does:

  1. The reference. The unrelated HGDP and 1000 Genomes samples the qc ancestry PCA was trained on (gnomAD v3.1 HGDP+1KG, CC0) were scored with the same variant rules as your sample, on pgsc_calc's own HGDP+1kGP reference genotypes. That panel lacks 149 of the 3,129 samples, all 28 Oceanians among them (gnomAD's sample QC), so 2,980 remain: AFR 605, AMR 361, CSA 603, EAS 667, EUR 607, MID 137. There is no Oceanian group. Only the scoring-file rows the reference has (the calibration set) count toward the calibrated score; the published reference file holds those rows, their reference allele frequencies, the reference samples' scores and the fitted models, not genotypes or scoring files. It is the GitHub release pgs-ref-2 (30 MB), downloaded into <library>/pgs/ only with --download, and checked against a pinned SHA-256. Each score is tied to the SHA-256 of the Catalog file it was built from; if the Catalog changes that file, or you supply your own, there is no percentile.
  2. Your ancestry. Your genotypes at the ~187,000 ancestry sites are projected onto the reference's 10 principal components (the qc ancestry PCA; qc ancestry reports the same ancestry_pc1…ancestry_pc10). Site- or sample-filtered calls are left out. With reference evidence (a gVCF input, or the gVCF the VCF's caller published), an absent site is homozygous reference only where the gVCF calls it callable reference; ancestry_calibration.pca_missing_site_states counts the absent sites' states. Without it, a whole-genome variants-only VCF's absent sites count as homozygous reference. Either way, only inside --confident-bed when one is given.
  3. The adjustment (pgscatalog.calc's methods, reimplemented):
    • Adjusted percentile (headline): Z_norm2, your score minus a mean and divided by an SD that both depend on PCs 1-4, fitted jointly on the reference (Khan et al. 2022, Nat Med; eMERGE GIRA), and its normal percentile.
    • z_norm1: only the mean adjusted (Khera et al. 2019, Circulation).
    • Empirical percentile: your score's percentile among the reference samples of the region you are most similar to (Mahalanobis distance on PCs 1-5: AFR, AMR, CSA, EAS, EUR or MID), when that match isn't low-confidence (p < 1e-10).

Calibration rows your VCF has no genotype for are filled in with the mean dosage of the reference region you are most similar to, as pgsc_calc fills in missing genotypes with reference frequencies. The error this adds (imputation_error_sd, in units of Z_norm2's fitted SD at your PCs) is the imputed rows' variance under that region's frequencies, inflated by the score's linkage disequilibrium there (the reference samples' score variance over the summed per-variant variance). Assuming the imputed rows share the whole score's LD makes it an upper bound for scores of many correlated variants: on real HG002 and HG005 gaps it is 0.06-0.18 SD where the measured error is 0.05-0.16 SD, and without the inflation it would be half the measured error for PGS000027 (version 1 of the reference, #617). percentile_interval is the resulting 95% interval. The percentile is withheld (percentile_status, and a pgs_uncalibrated warning saying why) when:

percentile_statusMeaning
calibratedreported (not withheld)
below_match_thresholdunder --min-match of the scoring rows matched
reference_calibration_unavailablenot a curated Catalog file on GRCh38 (custom files, uncurated IDs, GRCh37)
reference_unavailablea curated file, but the HGDP+1KG reference couldn't be downloaded or verified (the reason says why)
calibration_coverage_too_lowmore than 5% of the calibrated score's variance would be filled in
ancestry_projection_unavailablethe VCF can't give ancestry genotypes (variants-only, not a whole genome, and without reference evidence)
ancestry_projection_incompleteunder 90% of the ancestry sites have a genotype
ancestry_outside_referenceyour PCs are outside the reference samples' range (p < 1e-10)

When it is reported, confidence is medium, or low with a pgs_ancestry_mismatch warning when the score was developed in ancestries other than the reference region you are most similar to. The development ancestry is pinned with each calibrated scoring file, so it holds for a --scoring-file copy without the Catalog's metadata. It is never high: a percentile says where your score falls among reference genomes, not your risk, and a score predicts less well outside the ancestry it was developed in, whatever the adjustment. At less than --min-match (default 0.75), pgs_low_match also warns, and confidence is low.

The JSON's ancestry_calibration holds the calibrated score, calibration counts and imputed variance share, the PCs, the most similar population and its similarity, the empirical percentile and Z, z_norm1, z_norm2, imputation_error_sd and percentile_interval. The manifest's metrics add percentile and z_adjusted; the TSV adds z_adjusted and percentile_status.

Validation (#617, on lin-big, 2026-11-14, for version 1's 11 scores; version 2 copies ten of them unchanged):

  • Same rules as your sample. For 3 reference samples and all 11 scores, score pgs's own scorer, run on their genotypes exported as a VCF, gives the reference sums to 2.7e-6 (plink2's printed precision).
  • Same adjustment as pgscatalog.calc 0.3.1. Given the same reference, PCs and scores, compare_ancestry (Mahalanobis) and pgs_adjust agree on the most similar region for every one of the 962 reference samples outside the training set and the GIAB samples, on Z_norm1 and Z_norm2 within 0.0024, and on the empirical percentile exactly (apart from ties within rounding). pgscatalog.calc's default RandomForest picks the same region for 97%.
  • Calibrated out of sample. Across those 962 samples, Z_norm2 has mean within ±0.3 and SD 0.82-1.10 in each region (MID: 21 samples), and 92-99% fall within ±1.96.
  • GIAB VCFs. HG001's GIAB v4.2.1 VCF and confident BED give calibrated scores within 0.18 SD of its genotypes in the reference panel, as expected from its imputed share.

Version 2 (#1165, on macOS, 2026-11-22) rebuilt only bmi's PGS005198. The same build reproduces version 1's height sums exactly, and the same checks hold:

  • Coverage. 1,202,909 of its 1,217,710 rows (98.8%) are calibration rows.
  • Same rules as your sample. score pgs's scorer gives the reference sums of 3 reference samples to 3.9e-6.
  • Same adjustment as pgscatalog.calc 0.3.1. It agrees on the most similar region for all 962 out-of-training samples, on Z_norm1 and Z_norm2 within 0.0014, and on HG002 (Z_norm2 -2.302 against -2.303).
  • Calibrated out of sample. Z_norm2 has mean within ±0.1 and SD 0.90-1.09 in AFR, AMR, CSA, EAS and EUR, with 92-97% within ±1.96. MID's 21 samples have mean 0.55 (SD 1.17), and the score's development ancestries don't include MID, so such samples also get pgs_ancestry_mismatch.
